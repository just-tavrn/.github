# Project governance

## Official project and decisions

[tavrn](https://github.com/just-tavrn/tavrn) is the entry point to the official project. Its source and
release history remain public in the just-tavrn GitHub organization under the existing project licenses.
The maintainer, [babatonga](https://github.com/babatonga), currently makes final scope, release and access
decisions. Contributors retain copyright. There is no independent foundation or elected governing body.

Propose substantial changes in an issue or discussion before implementation. Protocol changes start in
tavrn-protocol; implementations follow. Changes go through pull requests and the repository checks.
As maintainers join, repository permissions and review responsibility will be documented publicly.
No contributor, sponsor or hosting customer acquires project control through a contribution or payment.
Forks are permitted under the licenses, but cannot claim to be official tavrn releases.

## Commercial services

The project owner may establish a company for managed hosting, subscriptions and support. Independent
providers may offer the same kinds of services without permission or a commercial software license.
The company receives no additional contribution rights under the CLA. Hosting charges pay for services;
they do not remove the software license obligations. Separate company services do not change where the
official open-source development happens. Any future change of project stewardship will be announced publicly.
The [trademark policy](https://github.com/just-tavrn/.github/blob/main/TRADEMARK.md) distinguishes truthful
advertising by independent providers from claims of official affiliation.

## License boundaries

The node is AGPL-3.0-only. Modified network deployments must offer the corresponding source to their users
as required by that license. Crypto and E2EE use MPL-2.0: source obligations generally apply upon distribution
and cover the MPL files. Web client, apps, SDK and bot code use Apache-2.0, which permits closed modifications.
Documents and protocol assets follow their repository license maps. There is no duty to submit upstream pull
requests, and independent programs are not automatically subject to the node's copyleft merely for using its API.
The CLA grants no general permission to relicense other people's work. Changing a license requires the
necessary rights and a separately announced decision; already granted licenses remain subject to their terms.
